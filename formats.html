Formats

Poetry first. Not poetry only.

ArtiStanza launches in poetry and prose because that is where the need is sharpest and the neglect is worst. But the reason a poem, a recording, and a gallery behave identically here is not that we thought ahead — it is that nothing in the system assumes a piece of work is text.

Every one of these runs on the same machinery. Free, member-locked, tier-locked, one-off purchase, chapter-by-chapter, monthly payouts. A new format inherits the entire paywall system for free. That is the whole point of designing it this way, and it is why the next format costs weeks rather than a rebuild.

Format one

Poetry, treated as poetry

A poem reflowed into a blog post isn't a poem. Line breaks are meaning, stanza breaks are meaning, and indentation is meaning. Every poetry platform gets this wrong because the underlying idea is "a block of text".

  • Line breaks and stanza breaks are preserved exactly as you typed them. No reflow, no justification, no auto-spacing.
  • Typography is yours. Font, size, line spacing, and margins, either yours or the reader's. Both settings persist per reader.
  • Stanzas are addressable. Because stanzas are stored as parts, you can lock one, free another, or reorder later without touching the poem's text.
  • Verse metadata if you want it. Form, meter and rhyme scheme are recorded as fields, so a sonnet is identifiable as a sonnet. Entirely optional.
  • Reading time is honest. Calculated for poetry at reading speed, not prose at 250 words a minute.
  • Collections get a proper page. Read in order, with each poem's access respected individually — members see the whole book, free readers see whichever poems you left open.

A poem, as it appears

Small Hours the kettle clicks off and the house begins to breathe again i am the only thing still deciding to be awake the dark is not empty, only unwitnessed — averyquill

Free · 8 lines · 1 minute · free verse

Line breaks intact, stanza breaks intact, sign-off intact. Not a blog post that happens to contain a poem.

Format two

Long work that pays for itself gradually

A three-part story almost never sells as one lump sum. Split into chapters with their own locks, a reader starts free, gets pulled in, and unlocks the rest over several weeks.

Chapters, not one lump

Each part carries its own lock. Free the first, lock the middle, price the ending separately if you want readers who'll only buy the last part.

Reading time up front

Stated before anyone commits, so a 40-minute story doesn't look like a 5-minute one. Honest expectations are what keep members from feeling ambushed.

The archive earns later

After a collection is out, unlock the middle chapters for members so the whole thing is readable. Old work keeps paying instead of sitting locked.

Sequential release also works. Publish one chapter a week, unlocked for members the moment it goes up. Serialisation is how a lot of short-story writers built an audience before subscriptions existed, and it works here too.

Everything after

The same three ideas, whatever the medium

Once the paywall system exists, a new format only has to answer one question: what does a reader need to see to understand this, and what should stay for members?

Format What a "chapter" is What's free What members get
Recorded reading One track — one poem, read aloud Text always free; a couple of tracks free The full recording, lossless option, liner notes
Gallery One series, or one image in a series A few images at full size High-resolution files, full series, early view
Music release One track Lead single, always Whole release early, liner notes, stems
Digital file One item — a zine, a PDF, an archive Sample pages or a low-resolution copy The file, full resolution, forever
Photography set One sequence A handful of frames as a public showing Everything, watermarked-free, print options

We tested this, rather than claiming it

The claim that a new format needs no rebuild is easy to make and easy to get wrong, so we built the literary site, then attached a format nobody had anticipated. It went live alongside everything else without altering a single existing piece of work. That is the difference between a plan and a fact.

Why it isn't a promise we might break

Because it isn't a feature we've promised to build. Every piece of work is already stored the same way regardless of format, so a new format is a player and a template — not a data migration, and not a risk to work you've already published.
