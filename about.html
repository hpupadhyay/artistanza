About

Someone who reads a lot of poetry got tired of the software

Not a mission statement. This is the actual reason, the actual state of things, and the parts I don't know yet.

Why

The origin, plainly

Every few months I go looking for a poem somebody mentioned. It takes twenty minutes. The writing is usually good and almost always free, but finding it means an archive, an account, an advert for a mattress, and a small article that scrolls away.

That's not a complaint about poetry sites. Several are excellent, and I'd rather someone reading this use one of those. The problem is that the tools were built to maximise time on a page rather than to keep a body of work findable and a writer paid.

So the question was: what would you build if you assumed the writer keeps their own archive forever and will eventually want to leave? Answer that honestly and most of the familiar features stop being features and start being threats.

The name. Arti and stanza. The two halves of a writer's life, which are apparently not the same job.

Where the idea came from

  • Small press chapbooks. Paying for a printed collection and getting a real object that still exists fifty years later. That's the model.
  • Letterboxd-style lists. The value is in the list, not the post. A platform that made the archive the primary unit would suit writers far better.
  • Bandcamp. The clearest precedent for fans paying artists directly without a middleman taking a cut of the meaning.
  • Subscription fatigue. Nobody has patience left for another subscription. So memberships have to be one at a time and easy to leave.

What this is not

  • Not a social network. There are no follower counts to optimise.
  • Not an AI writing tool. Nothing here generates work for you.
  • Not a self-publishing service. No file conversion, no ISBN, no print.
  • Not trying to replace presses, journals, or readings. It sits underneath them.

Being clear about that has saved more time than any feature so far.

Card handling, creator payouts, and the tax arithmetic that makes a small payout viable. Not one line of this is written, so treat the payout timing on the pricing page as intent.

Not started

The reading experience

Pages that reflow poetry without destroying line breaks, sync across devices, and stay readable offline. The other half of the product and the half nobody demos.

Being direct about this: the platform does not exist yet. What's done is the part that is hardest to change later and most likely to be got wrong by people who build quickly — how work is stored, and who is allowed to read it. Building that first was a choice, not an accident.

How it'll be run

Commitments, in order of importance

  • Reader relationships are not assets. No selling, renting, or sharing member lists, now or after an acquisition.
  • No ads. Not on writers' work, not on readers' feeds, not as a fallback if revenue disappoints.
  • Export is a feature, not an exit. One click, plain files, no fee, no retention prompt.
  • Grandfathering. If the fee changes, existing members keep their rate.
  • One subscription per reader. We'll never ask for two.
  • Small team, direct contact. You'll be able to reach an actual person about a problem.

The test I intend to apply to any decision: if a writer published their whole body of work here for five years and I shut the company down tomorrow, would they still have everything, and would they still be able to keep their readers?

If the answer is no, the feature doesn't ship, however good the numbers look.

On the money, plainly: ArtiStanza takes a percentage and nothing else. No listing fee, no pay-to-be-seen, no selling placement, no charging writers for the tools needed to reach their own audience.

The shortlist

Who should join, and who shouldn't

Good fit

  • Poets and writers who publish regularly or want to start.
  • People with a small existing audience from readings, workshops, or a chapbook.
  • Anyone planning a first or second collection and willing to fund it.
  • Writers whose work spans forms and are tired of maintaining one site per medium.
  • Readers who already pay for at least one thing and want to pay a writer instead of an advertiser.

Poor fit

  • People who need income from writing this month. There is no version of this that fixes that yet.
  • Anyone looking for a large audience without publishing regularly. No platform can supply that.
  • Writers who want an audience-building tool with engagement analytics and posting advice. Wrong product.
  • People who need print books, editing, or translation handled. We don't do those and won't pretend to.

If you're in the second list, the free tier on every existing platform is a perfectly good answer, and I'd rather you use it than feel pressured.
