How it works

Publish, gate, get paid

Three actions, and no separate paywall system to learn. The lock is a setting on the post itself, so it lives and dies with the post — it can't get left behind when you edit or unpublish something.

1

Write and publish

Drop in a poem, a story, a recording, a gallery. Save as draft, schedule it for a date, or post it now.

Drafts are private by default. Nothing is visible until you publish, and scheduling keeps a piece hidden until its moment.

2

Choose who sees it

Free for everyone, free for members only, locked to a tier you name, or sold individually. Change it whenever.

Nothing about a piece is permanent. Unlock an old poem for everyone after a collection is published, or lock last year's free work for new members.

3

Keep the money

Monthly and annual tiers, tips, and one-off sales. Regular payouts with a fee breakdown you can read.

Members cancel on their own terms, any time. You are not chasing anyone or begging anyone to stay.

Access rules

Four ways to open a door

You pick one per piece. Most creators use all four at once — free work to be found, member-only reading to sustain, tier-locked pieces to fund the tier, and occasional one-offs for the requests that come in.

SettingWho can open itGood for
Free Everyone. No account, no email, nothing asked. Your public voice. This is the work that earns new members.
Members Anyone holding any active tier of yours. Backlist reading, bonus pieces, community notes, unpolished drafts.
Tier locked Readers on a specific tier or anything above it. The work you most want funded: new collections, early access.
One-off Anyone who pays, once. They keep it permanently. A specific poem someone asked for. No ongoing commitment for them.

Upgrade once, unlock everything. Tiers are an ordered ladder. If a piece is locked to tier 2, then tier 3 members see it too — automatically, with no settings to update. You set the rule once and it holds for every future tier.

Locked pieces still appear in member feeds. Readers see the title, your note, and the free part, with a clear badge. That's not a bug — it's how someone discovers there is work they'd like to read. A post you hide completely is a post nobody can find.

Writing the teaser

The free part has to earn the rest

This is the single most important writing decision on the platform, and nobody can make it for you.

A reader decides whether to pay based entirely on what they got for free. Two failures show up repeatedly, and they are mirror images of each other.

Too little

The teasing first line

The gate opens after the title. The reader gets the feeling of being cut off, and pays out of irritation or suspicion. They read a sentence, feel cheated, and cancel within a month.

Too much

Almost the whole thing

You give away the entire poem and charge for the last stanza. Readers spot this instantly. It reads as a pricing error, and it makes the whole tier feel like a tax on the ending.

What works instead

  • Give a complete thing. The first stanza, or the opening movement — finished in itself, so it can be read as a small poem.
  • Make the locked part feel like an event. A turn, a reveal, the arrival. If the free part already resolves, nobody pays.
  • Set it by hand. Choose the exact split rather than a percentage. You know where each piece turns.
  • Test it once. Send the free part to a friend who reads a lot. If they aren't curious about the rest, the split is wrong.

Long work is where tiering earns the most. The pattern below is the one most creators settle on.

Longer work

Lock the chapters, not the book

A novel-length piece almost never sells as one lump. Splitting it lets a reader start free, get hooked, and unlock the rest over weeks — while giving you more chances to be paid for the same work.

The pattern most creators land on

  1. Chapter one free. Always. This is the shop window and it never costs you anything.
  2. Middle chapters at one tier. The bulk of the work, where the revenue actually lives.
  3. Final chapter one tier higher, or as a one-off purchase for people who'll only buy the ending.
  4. Once the book is out, unlock the middle for members so the whole thing is readable — the back catalogue keeps earning instead of gathering dust.

Because a lock lives on each chapter rather than the whole piece, changing your mind later is a per-chapter edit. You are never forced to choose once and live with it.

The same mechanism serves every format. For a recording, a locked "track" is one song. For a gallery, it's one image in a series. Nothing about the mechanism changes — only what a "chapter" means for the format you're working in.

In progress

Work in progress is a feature, not a leak

Private drafts

Visible only to you, and to any collaborators you invite. Drafts are never indexed, never emailed, never reachable by link.

Member-only drafts

Share the rough version with the people who pay you. Readers consistently say the members-only drafts are the thing they'd miss most if they left.

Scheduled

Set a date and time. The piece stays invisible until then, then appears automatically — no 6am publishing, no timezone maths.

One rule, enforced absolutely: a draft is never visible to the public, including if someone has the direct link. This is the one thing the system will not let you get wrong, because a leaked draft is the kind of mistake you cannot walk back.

What a normal month looks like

  • Week 2 — the same poem as a recording. Free for members, because the reading is a different piece of work from the text.
  • Week 3 — a finished poem, tier-locked. One free stanza, the rest at tier 2. This is the post that pays the bills.

  • Monthly — one tier-locked story, in chapters. Three parts, first free, collected into the paid back catalogue after a month.

Four pieces a month, three of them free, is a perfectly normal rhythm and it needs no marketing budget. The free work is the marketing; the locked work is the reason people stay.

Common questions

Can I change a lock later?
Yes, at any time, on any piece. It applies immediately to everyone. Existing members keep whatever they already unlocked.
What happens when someone downgrades?
They keep everything they bought outright. Work locked above their new tier becomes preview-only until they upgrade again. Nothing is deleted.
Do members cancel automatically?
No. They choose, and they get a reminder before anything is charged. We would rather lose a member than lose their trust.
Can readers buy without an account?
Yes. A one-off purchase unlocks the piece for anyone. An account is only needed if they want it kept in their library.
Can I repost work I published elsewhere?
Yes, and we don't ask for exclusivity. If it later appears in a print collection, unlock it here for members — your readers get the archive.
