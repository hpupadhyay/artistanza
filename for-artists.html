For artists

You should own the people who like your work

Not own rent. Own. The reader list, the emails, the archive, and the relationship. Everything below follows from taking that seriously.

What we commit to

Five things, in plain language

1

Your readers are yours

Full member list with names, emails and amounts, exportable in one click. Yours for as long as you have an account, whether or not you leave.

2

Your archive is yours

Every piece, in open formats, exportable at any time. Ordinary text and image files, not something only our software can read.

3

No ads, ever

No advertising on your work and no selling your reader list. If a business model ever required it, we'd have to be a different company.

4

No algorithm in the way

Nothing decides who sees your work. Your readers' feed is chronological, and anyone who follows you gets everything you publish.

5

No exclusivity

Publish here, in a magazine, and in a chapbook. We don't ask you to keep work exclusive, because your archive is more valuable if it's complete.

And one promise to be careful with

If our fee ever changes, existing members keep the rate they signed up at. We won't quietly reprice work you already sold.

The comparison

Against the alternatives

Publishing platforms and subscription platforms are both reasonable tools for reasonable jobs. They are simply the wrong shape for someone who writes.

The honest version: subscription platforms got a great deal right, and if you are already paying one of them and happy, you may not need this. The gap is that most of them are built for a writer who publishes regularly and in volume. For someone publishing a poem every few weeks, the per-piece controls and the verse handling are what make the difference.

Practicalities

The unglamorous part

What you'll actually spend time on

Roughly the same as any publishing platform: writing, and occasionally deciding what to give away.

  • Writing. Everything else is smaller than this.
  • Deciding the free part. Once per piece, and it's the decision that most affects whether you get paid.
  • Replying to members. Genuinely part of the job, and part of why people stay. Budget for it.
  • Consistency. Irregular posting loses members more than disappointing posts do.

Colleaborations

Where several people share a piece — a translation, an essay, a reading with music — everyone gets credited, everyone can be given access, and everyone appears on the work. No one is asked to hand over the post.

Getting paid outside the platform

If your work is also sold as a book, a print edition, or a commission, nothing on ArtiStanza affects that. Your member list and archive come with you.

Is this worth it for you?

ArtiStanza pays well if you already have readers somewhere and want to convert them. It pays slowly if you're starting from nothing, because no platform fixes that.

  • Good fit: you publish regularly, or have an audience from readings, workshops, or a small press.
  • Good fit: you want to publish a collection and fund the next one.
  • Poor fit: you're looking for a way to avoid publishing regularly.
  • Poor fit: you need income this month. Build the free tier and the locked work first; expect a year before it's dependable.

I'd rather tell you that now than take your money later.
