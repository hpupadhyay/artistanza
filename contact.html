Get early access

Tell me what you make

Founding access is small and deliberate. I'd rather read fifty replies properly than process five thousand signups, so this is a conversation rather than a queue.

Request an invite

No account, no password, no newsletter signup you didn't ask for. Just a reply to one email.

This opens your email client with the message prefilled, because there's no server behind this site yet. If you'd rather not, write to hello@artistanza.com directly.

What happens next

  1. A reply within a few days
    From an actual person, and usually with questions.
  2. A short conversation
    About what you write and how you publish now. Sometimes this ends there, and that's fine.
  3. An invite if it fits
    Access arrives in small groups as the editor becomes usable.

What I'm asking for

Not money, at this stage. Two things instead:

  • Publish honestly. Write something in public for three months, even if it's small, and tell me where the software got in your way.
  • Be blunt. The editor's default behaviour is the decision I most want to get wrong early, while changing it is still free.

Other ways in

  • Press: hello@artistanza.com
  • Reading series: same address, mention the venue and date.
  • Developers: read this first, then write with your actual concerns.

Nothing on this page is stored on a server, and nothing is tracked. When the real site exists, this form becomes the thing it should have been from the start.

Before you write

Reasonable questions

When does this launch?
I don't have a date, and giving you one would be dishonest. The foundation is done and the editor is the next piece of work. Invites go out when it's usable, not before, and you'll hear from me either way.
Can I join now and just read the free work?
Not yet, because there isn't anything to read. Reading access comes with the first group of invites, and free access will always be free once it exists.
Is it free to publish?
Yes. There's no listing fee and no cost to having an account. The only money that moves is when a reader chooses to pay you.
Do I need to be a poet?
No. Painters, musicians, translators, essayists, and anyone making something that reads sequentially all fit. "Artist" here means anyone who publishes their own work regularly.
What if I already use a subscription platform?
Keep it if it's working for you. The point isn't to replace something that isn't broken, it's to have an option for people who want to own their list and archive.
Will you reply to every message?
Yes, including critical ones. If you have a genuine objection to how this is built, that's the most valuable email I'll get this month.

Start with the hardest part

Every platform makes promises. This one has to earn them, and the only way I know of is finding out where it's wrong while it's still cheap to fix.
