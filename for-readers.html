For readers

Pay the poet, not the middleman

Reading is not broken because readers don't want to pay for words. It's broken because the money rarely travels back to the person who wrote them. This is an attempt at fixing the routing, not at convincing you to feel guilty.

The offer

What a membership actually gives you

1

A writer, not a feed

Follow someone and you get everything they publish, newest first. No ranking, no discovery lottery, no wondering why a post did badly.

2

Work that stays put

Everything you read stays readable. Collections don't vanish and old work doesn't disappear because a platform changed its mind.

3

You see what you're paying for

Every locked piece shows you the free part, the price, and exactly what your money unlocks before you decide.

4

Nothing to skip

No ads, no newsletter pop-ups, no "read more" between a poem and its end, no notification badges counting down to sell you something.

5

Close to the work

Drafts, early access, and live readings with the people you actually support. The closest thing to a writer's room you can get as a reader.

It's cheap for what it is

A tier 2 membership at $5 is less than one coffee, a month, and it funds someone making the thing you're about to read. Most of the free work on the platform costs you nothing at all.

Paywalls

We don't like paywalls either

A paywall is a legitimate way for a writer to get paid. A bad paywall is a wall with nothing behind it. Ours has a rule.

  • You always get a real teaser. A finished opening movement, not one dangling sentence. If we show you almost nothing, that's a bug and we want to hear about it.
  • You always know the price first. Stated on the post, before any sign-up, with no surprise currency conversion or fee appearing at checkout.
  • You can cancel yourself. Two clicks, no phone call, no retention survey, no "are you sure" theatre.
  • Anything you bought is yours. A one-off purchase stays unlocked permanently, even if you cancel a membership or your card expires.
  • Failed payments don't delete anything. A grace period and reminders. Your library survives a bad month.

If a piece isn't worth paying for, don't pay for it. Every creator here can see their own numbers. A writer who locks a lot and gets nobody through is going to fix their teasers, because we all want you to read.

Paying, in three options

  • Membership
    Monthly or annual. Cancel any time. Most readers start here.
  • One-off purchase
    Buy a single poem or story. Yours forever. No commitment at all.
  • Tip
    Any amount, no account required. Genuinely no strings.

No account needed to buy anything. We only ask you to make one if you want your purchases kept in one place.

The honest case

Why pay a poet at all

Nobody makes you. A poem will still be there for free somewhere, and you can always read for nothing here. So the argument is not about access.

  • Most poets are paid nothing. The standard outcome for literary work is no income. Paying a membership makes you the exception rather than the default.
  • It's the cheapest possible act of support. $5 a month is less than the cover price of one print collection, and it recurs.
  • You get more, not less. Paid membership adds drafts, early access, and live readings. Paying doesn't entitle you to something that already exists.
  • It redirects money, not just attention. A membership is money in someone's account. A like is not.

The honest counter

When not to bother

Some things aren't worth paying for, and we'd rather say so than have you resent us.

  • You read one poem a year. The free tier exists exactly for this. Nothing about it is degraded.
  • You can't afford it. Read the free work. Genuinely. Most of what's here will always be free.
  • You want to support a publisher. Sometimes the better route for a serious book is a press. We'll say so when it's relevant.
  • You're already paying for a subscription platform. You may well get on fine here too, and there's no reason to pay twice for the same poet.

Membership fatigue is real and we take that seriously. One unpaid subscription at a time is the most we'll ever ask of anyone.

Questions

From the reader's side

Can I read without paying anything?
Yes, and a great deal of the site is free forever. Every creator keeps a free tier open, no exceptions. Membership unlocks more; it doesn't unlock the essentials.
Do I need an account to buy a single poem?
No. Pay and read. We only ask for an account if you want the piece kept in your library or the receipt sent somewhere durable.
What happens to my stuff if I cancel?
Anything you bought with a one-off purchase stays yours, permanently. Work unlocked only through membership goes back to being preview-only. Your notes, highlights, and library are never deleted.
Can I cancel easily?
Yes. Two clicks from your account page, effective immediately, no retention survey. A cancellation rate we have to fight with would be a sign we'd built something people didn't want.
Why does the free part stop where it does?
That's the creator's decision, not ours, and it's deliberate. The free part is meant to be a complete piece of writing, not a fragment designed to make you angry. If a teaser feels stingy, tell us and we'll pass it on.
Will I be sold to anyone?
No. Your email is used to deliver your receipts, membership renewals, and the posts of writers you chose to follow. It is not sold, rented, or shared with advertisers. That is the business model, not a setting.
Can I read on a phone or offline?
Both are planned for launch. Reading progress syncs across your devices, and poetry reflows properly to whatever width you have without breaking the line breaks the poem depends on.
