Pricing

You name your price

There is no ArtiStanza subscription for readers and no listing fee for creators. The numbers below are worked examples from a poet with about 200 readers — yours will be different, and the structure is what matters.

Worked example

Three tiers, doing three different jobs

Tier 1 exists to be free and always will be. Tiers 2 and 3 do the actual work: one covers the reading habit, the other funds the work itself.

Order is the whole trick. Tier 3 outranks tier 2, so anything locked to tier 2 opens for tier 3 members automatically. When you add a fourth tier next year, every existing lock keeps working and your new members see everything. You will never sit down to re-pricing.

Getting it right

Why not just have one paid tier?

One paid tier is simpler to build and much worse for you. It forces a choice between readers who will pay a little and readers who will pay a lot, and you end up pricing for the smaller of the two groups.

  • A low tier catches people who'd otherwise pay nothing. Most readers who will ever pay will pay $5, not $20. The low tier is what converts them.
  • A high tier funds the ambitious work. Ten people paying $18 is $180 a month — enough for a month of not doing anything else, which is the actual cost of writing a collection.
  • Each tier is a promise with a price attached. "Members-only drafts" is a promise. "Members-only drafts plus a monthly Q&A" is a product. Different people want different amounts of closeness.
  • You can run the platform on tier 2 alone. The high tier is upside, not a dependency. That is the difference between a business and a gamble.

Don't over-build the ladder. Three tiers is right for most people. Five means members can't tell the difference and you spend all your energy on tier descriptions instead of writing.

Also supported

  • Annual billing at a discount — typically two months free, which improves retention more than any price cut.
  • One-time lifetime tiers for people who dislike subscriptions.
  • Free trials, with the card only charged if they stay.
  • Founding-member pricing locked permanently for your earliest supporters.
  • Pay-what-you-want with a minimum, for a season or a collection.
  • Comp access for press and friends, with an expiry date.

The low month is the common one. Most memberships take a year of steady posting to reach the steady column, and the good month is rare. If the low month doesn't cover your costs, this is a supplement, not a salary.

What actually moves the numbers

Not the tier prices — the number of tier-2 members. Doubling that column is a year of consistent publishing. Chasing it with promotions mostly buys you churn.

We will not pretend otherwise: most poets will not make a living here. What this platform offers is a direct line to the readers who already love your work — something no feed can give you, because the feed keeps them precisely by not telling them you exist.

Fees and money

One fee, stated plainly

  • 10% of what you earn. That's it. Memberships, one-off sales and tips all use the same rate.
  • Card processing is ours. We absorb it rather than passing it on as a second charge you have to explain to readers.
  • Payouts monthly, on any threshold you like, to a bank account or card.
  • A change in our fee never applies retroactively. Existing members keep the rate they signed up at.
  • No listing fee, no setup fee, no monthly minimum.

Where the 10% goes

Payments and fraud handling, storage and bandwidth for your files, email delivery, the site itself, and the staff of one. Nobody here is funded by venture capital, so the fee covers the work.

We'd rather be a sustainable 10% than a temporary 5% that funds growth spending and then becomes 15%. You can see the whole bill because there is only one line of it.

Getting paid

How money reaches you

1

Reader pays

Card, and local payment methods where the reader lives. They see the full price and the full description before anything is charged.

2

It clears

Card payments take a few days to settle. Access unlocks immediately; the money moves after settlement, which is why new tiers wait before payout.

3

Fee is split

Gross, platform fee and your net are recorded separately on every single transaction, and you can reconcile them to the penny.

4

You're paid

Monthly, automatically, to your bank or card. A CSV of the whole ledger is always one click away.

Taxes: where sales tax or VAT applies, we collect it and remit it. What reaches you is what you expected to receive, not a surprise deduction. If your situation needs specific handling — a business account, a different tax residence — that's a question for early access, and we'll work it through before you publish.

Questions

Money questions worth asking

Do I need to be registered as a business?
No. Individual creators are supported, and the setup is a few fields rather than a legal entity. If you do have a business, or an LLC for other work, payouts go straight to it and nothing changes for your readers.
Who pays the card fee?
We do. A reader sees one number — your price — and never a line item about surcharges. A membership that quietly costs more at checkout than it advertised is a membership that gets cancelled.
What happens if a reader's payment fails?
They keep access for a grace period while we retry, and they get several reminders. Their access only ends if they do nothing. We never quietly delete a reader's library, and one-off purchases they already paid for stay unlocked permanently regardless.
Can I change my prices later?
Whenever you like, for new members. Existing members keep their rate. If you raise a price, nobody's membership is cancelled — existing members are grandfathered and new ones pay the new figure.
Is there a cap on how much I can earn?
No cap on income. Payment providers have anti-fraud thresholds they enforce regardless of who we are, and we'll tell you plainly if you approach one rather than letting a payment fail silently.
Can I pay for my own membership?
You can, and collaborators can buy access to a shared project. But it won't count toward your member numbers, because it isn't a reader relationship. We'd rather the analytics stayed honest.
What if I want to leave?
Take your archive and your member list. Nothing is held hostage, and we don't require notice. We'd rather leave you well than keep you resentfully.

Start with a free tier

The free tier costs nothing and always will. Publish in public first, see who shows up, and add a paid tier when you have something worth unlocking.
